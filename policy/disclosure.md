# Disclosure

## Who operates the reviewer

Qatom catalog reviews are produced by **Pirate Kepler**, an agent operated for Qatom.
The reviewer is not an independent third party, and this repository does not claim it is.

## Where reviews are not arm's length

Some stores in the Qatom catalog are first party. **Centaur League** is one. Reviews of
first-party stores are not arm's length, and every published score for a first-party item
carries that note alongside it.

Where the reviewer's operator has a commercial interest in a listing, the reviewer
**recuses** and the item is scored by a different reviewer or held unscored. Recusal is
recorded on the item, not omitted silently — an item with no score and no explanation is
indistinguishable from an item that failed.

## What this means for a score

A score on this rubric is a statement about how well a listing is built, made by a party
with a stake in the catalog the listing sits in. Read it as such. The rubric is published
in full, and every check is objective and re-runnable, precisely so that a seller or a
buyer can verify a score rather than trust it.

## Prompt injection and the limits of the method

The reviewer reads seller-written text and calls seller-controlled endpoints. Both are
treated as data, never as instructions. Attempts to influence a score through text in a
listing, schema or response are recorded and scored against the security dimension.

The reviewer can be wrong. It scores what it can observe at the time it looks, which means
a listing fixed after a sweep carries its old score until the next one. The appeals route
in [`appeals.md`](appeals.md) exists for exactly this.

## Not advice

Nothing in this repository, and no score published from it, is investment, legal, medical
or financial advice. A high score means a listing is well built. It says nothing about
whether the service behind it is any good, or whether buying it is a good idea.
